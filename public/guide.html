Welcome to Pop OS

Pop OS is Pop Group's internal operating system — a single place to see every active project, who's working on what, where the money is, and what needs attention today. This guide covers login, your role's access, and the sections most relevant to your work.

Financial Dashboard

Click Financial in the top navigation to open the Finance Dashboard. This is designed specifically for the finance view — the first thing you see is the money position.

Toolbar actions — top right of the page

↓ Sync from Autocount

Pulls every quotation and invoice raised in Autocount this calendar year and copies their current amount, due date, and status into Pop OS. Existing documents are updated in place (matched by document number); documents that don't exist in Pop OS yet are created.

Use this when: someone edited or marked a document paid directly inside Autocount instead of through Pop OS, or a document was raised in Autocount without ever going through the "Push quotation" / "Push invoice" buttons here. It's a one-way pull — Autocount's data always wins and overwrites whatever Pop OS currently shows for that document. Reports the count synced: e.g. "12 quotations + 8 invoices: 2 created, 18 updated, 0 skipped."

⚖ Reconcile

A read-only health check — for every document Pop OS knows about, it looks that exact document number up live in Autocount and compares the amount and status Pop OS has on file against what Autocount actually shows right now. Nothing is changed by this button; it only reports differences.

Each document is classified:

✓ MatchAmount and status agree — nothing to do.
Amount ≠Same status, different amount (e.g. Autocount was edited after the fact).
Status ≠Same amount, different status (e.g. paid in Autocount, still shows Active here).
Both ≠Amount and status both disagree.
Not foundPop OS has this document, but no matching document number exists in Autocount at all — check for a typo'd doc number or a document deleted on the Autocount side.

The results table sorts mismatches to the top so the documents worth attention surface first. This does not fix anything automatically — if a mismatch is real, either update the record on the correct side manually, or run Sync from Autocount above to pull Autocount's version into Pop OS.

✉ Send alert email

Sends one digest email listing every active invoice that's overdue or due within the alert window (10 days by default) — document number, client, amount, and how many days until (or past) due. Requires SMTP to be configured in the server's .env (SMTP_HOST, SMTP_USER, SMTP_PASS, ALERT_EMAIL_TO) — if it isn't, the button tells you so instead of failing silently. There's no automatic schedule for this yet — it only sends when someone clicks the button.

↓ Export AR CSV

Downloads every accounting document Pop OS has on record — quotations, sales invoices, and purchase invoices, every status, not just what's overdue — as a spreadsheet: doc number, type, project, client, amount, doc date, due date, credit term, and status. Useful for a full export to hand to an accountant or auditor, or to build a pivot table outside Pop OS.

Top row — 4 KPI cards

Outstanding AR

Sales Pipeline & Leads

Click Sales ▾ → Sales pipeline to open the lead board. This is where every opportunity is tracked from first contact through to a won deal — visible to Sales and Admin.

Stats strip

Above the board: active lead count, total pipeline value (all leads not yet Won/Lost), won count, and closed value. Updates live as leads move stage or amounts change.

Adding a lead

Use the add-lead form above the board. Fill in:

NameRequired — the opportunity or project name.
AccountThe client company, picked from Clients. Optional at creation — a lead can start with no account and be linked later.
StatusStarting pipeline stage — usually Qualification.
PriorityLow / Medium / High / Very High — shown as a coloured label on the card.
Estimated amountExpected deal value (RM). Optional — can be left blank and filled in later.
Closed byThe salesperson responsible for this lead.

The pipeline board

Leads are shown as cards in five columns — drag isn't needed, each card has its own controls:

Qualification Proposal Negotiation Won Lost

Move stage — use the status dropdown on the card. Moving a lead to Won stamps the date it was won, used later for quarterly commission reporting.

Edit the estimated amount anytime — click the RM field on the card, type the new figure, then click away. It saves immediately, no separate save button. Leaving it blank clears the amount.

Reassign Closed By — change the salesperson dropdown on the card at any time.

Search — the search box above the board filters by lead name or account name across all columns as you type.

Delete — the × on a card removes the lead permanently. Use with care.

Converting a Won lead into a project

Once a lead is set to Won, a "→ Create project" button appears on its card. Click it to create a new project in BRIEF status, carrying over the name, client account, estimated value, and company. Any quotations already pushed to Autocount for this lead are automatically linked to the new project. A lead can only be converted once — after that the card shows "✓ Project created" instead.

Pushing a quotation

On a Won lead, use "↑ Push quotation" to create the quotation directly in Autocount — see Autocount Integration for the full walkthrough.

Announcements in Mattermost

An admin can have Pop OS post to Mattermost when a lead is created or moves to another stage — optionally only for Won / Lost, with or without the estimated value, and @mentioning whoever closed the deal. See Mattermost Notifications.

Every change is logged

Editing a lead — including the estimated amount — writes an entry to the Audit Log (Admin tab, Admin only), showing exactly which field changed, the old and new value, who made the change, and when.

Projects

Click Production ▾ → Projects. This is the spine of the system — every brief, its priority, its team, its budget, and its billing all live on one project record.

Two ways to view the list

List view

A sortable table — click any column header to sort by it. Use the Columns picker to show or hide fields you don't need. Search and filter by producer, status, or priority above the table.

Kanban view

Cards grouped into six status columns — Brief, In Progress, Internal Review, On Hold, Delivered, Cancelled. Drag a card to a new column to change its status; the same search/producer/priority filters apply here too.

Adding a project

Use the add-project form to create one directly, or convert a Won lead from the Sales tab — either way it starts in Brief status. A project can optionally be linked to a Client account, which is what makes it show up under that client's detail view.

The project detail page — click any project name to open it

Everything about a project lives in one place. Some pieces have their own dedicated write-ups elsewhere in this guide — linked below instead of repeated.

Priority & QuadrantEst. value, client tier, margin target, complexity — see PPM — Project Priority Matrix.
Drain gateTwo-signature approval, only shown when quadrant = Drain — also covered in the PPM section.
Required SkillsTag what the project needs and get ranked staff suggestions — see Staff Suggestions.
Team this weekWho's allocated to this project right now, pulled live from the Capacity board. A "Manage in Capacity" link jumps straight there, pre-filtered to this project.
AssetsDeliverable count and stage summary, with a link into the Assets board filtered to this project. See Assets.
Change RequestsCount of pending/approved/rejected requests against this project. See Change Requests.
Project CostsAdd cost lines — Warm Pool, Supplier, or Additional — with a description and amount. These feed both the Financial Dashboard's margin numbers and the net profit used in Sales Performance commission calculations.
Accounting DocumentsPush an invoice to Autocount, and mark any document Paid or Void. See Autocount Integration.

Deleting a project

Removes the project and everything attached to it — assets, capacity allocations, change requests, cost lines. There's no undo. Consider setting status to Cancelled instead if you want to keep the record.

Capacity Board

Click Production ▾ → Capacity. One row per person, per project, per week — this is how the studio tracks who's working on what, and how full each person's week is.

Adding an allocation

Use the week navigator (← →) at the top to pick the week, then fill in the form: person, project, role (Main or Support), and % of the week (1–100 normally).

Going over 100%

A person's allocations for one week normally can't add up past 100%. If a new allocation would push them over, a "Weekend approved by producer" checkbox appears — ticking it raises the cap to 140% for that week (100% weekday + up to 40% covering Saturday/Sunday). This is a deliberate check, not a formality — it exists so weekend work always has a visible producer sign-off attached to it.

Editing and removing allocations

Change the % — click the percentage next to any allocation, type the new value, then click away. It saves immediately. If the new value would push the person over 100% for the week, a confirmation appears asking whether to approve weekend work — same rule as adding a new allocation, just triggered after the fact.

Remove — the Remove button on any row deletes that allocation.

STAFF role sees the board read-only — no add form, no edit, no remove.

Grouping the board

Two buttons above the board switch how rows are grouped:

By Person

Each person's row shows their real total — a percentage of their week, with a bar scaled against 100% (or 140% once weekend work is present).

By Project

Each project's row shows a headcount instead of a percentage — summing % across different people isn't a meaningful "percent of" anything, so it isn't shown as one.

Click a group's header row to collapse or expand it — useful once the board has more than a handful of people or projects. Collapse all / Expand all toggles every visible group at once.

Unallocated this week

A strip below the board lists everyone active with zero allocations for the selected week — the fastest way to see who's free right now.

Weekly board in Mattermost

An admin can have this board posted to a Mattermost channel every week (for example Monday 09:00) — per person, per project, and who has spare capacity, for this week or next, limited to a company or departments. See Mattermost Notifications.

Assets

Click Production ▾ → Assets. Every deliverable a project produces — a video cut, a key art, an animation — is tracked here as it moves through production.

The five stages

Brief WIP Internal Review Revision Final Delivery

The board shows one column per stage. Move an asset forward (or back) using the stage dropdown on its card — there's no drag-and-drop here, just the dropdown.

CD sign-off at Internal Review

While an asset sits at Internal Review, a "CD signed off" checkbox appears on its card. This is a soft gate — it's a visible record of creative approval, but checking it (or leaving it unchecked) does not itself block or allow moving the asset to Revision or Final Delivery. The stage dropdown always works regardless. Use it as the studio's agreed signal to move forward, not as an enforced lock.

Other fields on each card

Assignee — who's working on it. Changing this is what makes the asset show up in that person's My Work tab.

Review link — paste a Drive / Frame.io / preview URL. Saves automatically when you click away from the field.

Filtering & adding

Filter the board to one project using the dropdown above it. Add a new asset from the left-hand form (name, project, description, assignee) — hidden for STAFF, who can update existing assets but not create or delete them.

Production Lanes

Click Production ▾ → Lanes. This is a read-only operating-rules view — every active project is automatically placed into one of four lanes based on its PPM quadrant, each with its own way of working. To move a project to a different lane, change its quadrant on the project's detail page — see PPM — Project Priority Matrix.

Gold quadrant

Template Factory

High value, manageable complexity. Move fast using proven processes.

  • Brief-to-kickoff within 48 hours
  • Use existing SOP templates — no reinvention
  • CD reviews at Internal Review gate only
  • Single revision round; scope-lock before production
Strategic Bet quadrant

Innovation Lab

High value, high complexity. Invest time — involve the CD from the start.

  • Extended brief phase (minimum 1 week)
  • CD joins the kickoff meeting
  • Weekly producer check-ins throughout
  • Two internal review rounds expected
Operational Filler quadrant

Automated Stream

Lower value, manageable complexity. Keep lean — self-serve where possible.

  • PM manages independently; no weekly check-ins needed
  • Single internal review round
  • Director sign-off not required unless the client requests it
  • Batch similar briefs where possible to save setup time
Drain quadrant

Gated Review

High risk — low return, high effort. Both approvals required before any work starts.

  • Exec + Producer BOTH approve before production begins
  • Scope locked in writing before kickoff
  • Weekly cost review against budget
  • First sign of scope creep → escalate to Producer immediately

Each project card also shows priority, company, deadline (flagged if overdue), producer, and an asset progress summary. Drain-quadrant cards additionally show the two gate approvals at a glance.

Change Requests

Click Production ▾ → Change Requests. This exists so a scope change from the client goes through a formal record instead of being quietly absorbed into the project — every request, its budget impact, and its outcome is kept as a permanent log.

Logging a request

Fill in: project, title, description of what's being asked for, budget impact in RM (leave blank if there's no cost impact), and optionally who requested it (e.g. a client contact name).

Approving or rejecting

Every pending request shows Approve and Reject buttons. Either one opens a small note field — optional on approve, useful for recording any conditions; effectively a reason on reject. Once decided, the request moves to Approved or Rejected and the buttons disappear.

Pending Approved Rejected

Filtering & the stats strip

Filter tabs (All / Pending / Approved / Rejected) sit above the list; a project dropdown narrows to one project. The stats strip totals every request and sums the budget impact of everything currently Approved.

Who can do what

Admin, Producer, and PM can log, approve, reject, and delete requests. Team Lead and Finance see the same list and stats, read-only.

Sales Performance & Commissions

Click Sales ▾ → Performance. Admin only. This is the commission report — how each producer is tracking against their quarterly target, and what they've earned.

How commission is actually calculated

  1. Revenue = sum of estimated value across every lead this producer closed as Won or Completed in the selected period (by the date it was marked Won).
  2. Net profit = revenue minus every Project Cost line (Warm Pool / Supplier / Additional) logged against each deal's linked project.
  3. Attainment % = revenue ÷ the producer's target for the period, as a percentage.
  4. Tier = the highest commission tier whose threshold the attainment has reached (e.g. reaching 80% qualifies for the 75% tier, not the 100% one).
  5. Commission = net profit × that tier's rate — using this producer's personal rate override for the tier if one is set, otherwise the global tier rate. If net profit is zero or negative, commission is RM 0.

Choosing a period

Pick a year, then YTD or a specific quarter (Q1–Q4). The Yearly Overview panel above the leaderboard always shows all four quarters plus the full-year total side by side, regardless of which period is selected below it.

Reading a producer's card

The attainment bar is scaled to 150% with markers at the 50/75/100/150% tier thresholds, so you can see at a glance which tier a producer is sitting in.

A "Custom rates" badge means this producer has at least one personal tier-rate override; otherwise the badge shows their current global tier.

"Show deal breakdown" expands every deal counted in the period, with a + cost button to log a cost line against that deal's project right from here.

Settings panel (next to the leaderboard)

Per-person commission ratesOverride the global rate for one producer at one tier. Select the producer, enter a custom % at any tier, Save. Clear to revert to the global rate.
Commission tiers (global)The default rate schedule — threshold (fraction of target, e.g. 0.75 = 75%) and rate (fraction of net profit, e.g. 0.025 = 2.5%) per tier, plus a label. Editable inline.
Sales targetsSet a producer's Q1–Q4 targets for a year in one go. Existing targets for that producer/year are listed below with a × to remove one.

People & Skills

Click HR ▾ → People / ELC. This is the staff record — everyone the studio can schedule, their skills, and (for admins) their login accounts. User (a login) and Person (a staff record) are separate things that can be linked together — that link is what powers My Work and the sign-off queue for that person.

Master lists (admin-managed)

Four studio-wide lists live on the left of this page and feed dropdowns everywhere else in Pop OS:

Skills

Grouped by category. Used for skill ratings and Required Skills tagging.

Job titles

Shown in the "Job Title" dropdown when adding a person.

Departments

Same idea — feeds the department dropdown.

Software

Tag people with the tools they use (e.g. Houdini, Premiere).

Skill ratings — 1 to 5

1Awareness — knows the concept, hasn't produced independently
2Beginner — can attempt with guidance; output needs review
3Practitioner — delivers solid work independently
4Advanced — handles complex work, mentors others
5Expert — studio authority, sets the standard

A rating is never just overwritten — every change is recorded with a source: Interview (the very first rating), Project Completion, or Manual Adjustment (requires a short note explaining why). Click a skill chip on a person's row to see the full history and change it.

The lock icon — login accounts

Filled green = this person has a login. Outline grey = they don't. Admins can click it to view the existing account, or create a new one (email, password, role) right from this row — that new login is automatically linked to this Person record.

Sign-off authority (✔ icon)

A per-person flag, independent of role — toggle it to grant or revoke the ability to approve/reject assets in the My Work sign-off queue. Typically the Creative Director and 3D Director.

New Joiner — the fast path

Creates the Person record and their login account together in one form, and sends them a welcome email with their credentials (if SMTP is configured). Faster than adding the person, then separately using the lock icon.

When someone leaves — Remove button

Opens a choice, not an immediate delete:

Warm pool

Left on good terms — may return as freelance/contract.

Resigned

Quit voluntarily.

Terminated

Employment ended by the company.

All three just change status — the record and its full history stay. A separate Hard delete option on the same screen permanently removes the person and everything attached to them; use it only for test or duplicate records.

Skill self-assessments (admin only)

Staff can rate their own skills through a separate public form (linked as "Open form ↗" on this page) without needing a Pop OS login. Submissions land here as Pending. If the submitter's name doesn't match an existing Person, match them manually before approving. Approve applies every rating in the submission to that person's record (each recorded with source = Manual Adjustment); Reject discards it.

Committees

Click HR ▾ → Committees. Tracks internal committees (Health & Safety, Social, etc.) — who's on them, in what role, and what they've actually done.

Creating a committee (admin only)

Name and an optional purpose. Everyone with access to this tab can view committees and their activity; only admins can create, archive, or delete one, add/remove members, or record activities.

Members

Add any full-time staff member not already active on the committee, with a role and join date:

Chair Secretary Member

Step down marks a member as former as of today — it doesn't delete them, so their tenure stays on record and they can rejoin later. Former members are tucked under a collapsible "Former members" line.

Activities & attendance

Record an activity with a title, date, notes (what was discussed), and outcome (decisions/action items). "Edit" attendance on any activity opens a checklist of current members to mark who actually attended.

Archiving vs deleting

Archive keeps the whole record but marks the committee inactive (reversible — Reactivate brings it back). Delete permanently removes the committee and every member/activity record attached to it.

Staffing Board

Click HR ▾ → Staffing. Pick a project and a week, and see every active staff member ranked by how free they are that week — skills are shown for context but don't affect the ranking.

Not the same thing as "Staff Suggestions"

This tab and the Staff Suggestions panel on a project's detail page share a similar name but do different jobs. This board shows everyone, sorted purely by availability for one specific week — no skill filtering, no scoring formula. The project-detail panel instead ranks only people with a required skill for that project, scored by skill level and average free capacity across the whole project window. Use this board for "who's free this week"; use Staff Suggestions for "who should I put on this project."

Reading the results

People already allocated to this project this week always sort to the top, so you immediately see who's already on it.

Everyone else sorts by free capacity — 100% minus whatever they're already committed to across all projects that week.

Each candidate's current skill ratings are listed for reference.

Admin & Audit Log

Click Admin in the top navigation. Admin only. Every create, update, and delete across the important tables — Leads, Projects, People, Users, Change Requests, Accounting Documents, and Mattermost notification rules — is logged here automatically and can never be edited or deleted. This is the answer to "who changed this, and what did it look like before." The Admin page also holds Mattermost Notifications, above the log.

Filters

YearDefaults to the current year. Pick "All years" to search the full history — can be a large result set.
ResourceLead, Project, Person, User, Change Request, Accounting Document, or Notification Rule.
ActionCreate, Update, or Delete.
ActorWhich user made the change.
Date rangeFrom / To dates — auto-filled to the selected year, but freely editable.
SearchFree text — matches against the record's name (project name, person name, etc).

Reset clears every filter back to the current-year default.

Viewing a change in detail

Each row shows when, who, what action, on what resource, and — if it was an update — how many fields changed. Click View to expand: for an update, every changed field is shown as old value → new value; the full raw record state as it was left after the change is always available underneath.

Results are paginated 50 rows at a time. Writing to the log never blocks the action that triggered it — if logging itself fails, the underlying create/update/delete still goes through.

Mattermost Notifications

Pop OS can post messages into Mattermost for you — to a channel, or as a private message to one person — so the team hears about things without opening Pop OS. Click Admin in the top navigation; the Mattermost Notifications panel is at the top of the page. Admin only. Messages are sent by a bot account called pop-os.

The pill at the top right of the panel shows ● Mattermost connected when everything is set up. If it says ○ Not configured, the server hasn't been connected to Mattermost yet — ask whoever looks after the server (the setup steps are in the project README).

The messages you can send

Weekly capacity boardSent on a day and time you choose (Malaysia time, GMT+8). Shows how booked everyone is for the week.
Lead createdSent the moment a new lead is added to the Sales pipeline.
Lead status changedSent the moment a lead moves to another pipeline stage — for example when a deal is won.

Setting up a rule

Each message you want sent is a rule. You can have as many as you like — for example, the capacity board to one channel and Won deals to another.

1

Click + New rule. Give it a name you will recognise, and choose the Message.

2

Choose what goes in it (see the two tables below). For the capacity board also pick the day and time — always Malaysia time.

3

Under Send to, add one or more recipients — a Channel or a Person (DM). Mix as many as you need.

4

Click Save rule, then Send test on the rule to check it reaches the right place before relying on it.

Recipients

Channel Type the channel's web-address name, not its display name. Open the channel in Mattermost and read the address bar: …/channels/pxl-resource-planning — the last part is what you type. The pop-os bot must be a member of the channel (private channels too), otherwise Pop OS reports "channel not found".
Person (DM) Pick a Pop OS user; they get a private message from the bot. Pop OS finds their Mattermost account using the email they log in with. If that email is different in Mattermost, type their Mattermost @username in the box beside the name.

Weekly capacity board — what you can choose

SectionsTick any combination. Per person — each person's total % with their project split and a flag (✅ full, 🟡 free capacity, 🔴 over 100%, 🟠 over 100% with weekend work approved). Per project — who is on each project and the total committed. Available people — everyone under 90% booked, most free first.
Week shownThis week or next week. Two rules give you, say, a Friday "next week" preview and a Monday recap.
Only include people fromEveryone, or one company. It looks at the person's company, so someone from LPS working on a PXL project still shows their full workload. People marked Group, or with no company, are always included.
DepartmentsTick the departments to include; none ticked means all. Tip: leave out departments that don't take production work (for example management), or they will show up as "100% free".
People with nothing bookedTick to list active people who have no allocation that week, so gaps are visible.

Lead messages — what you can choose

Stages (status changed)Only be told when a lead moves to the stages you tick — for example just Won and Lost. None ticked means every stage.
Only include leads fromAll companies, or one. Leads marked Group, or with no company, are always included.
Show the estimated valueUntick this if the channel has more people in it than should see deal sizes.

PPM — Project Priority Matrix

The PPM engine helps producers decide which quadrant a project belongs in and how high it should be prioritised, based on four numbers you fill in on the project detail page. Once you have enough project history, an AI layer also kicks in and learns from past decisions.

Step 1 — Fill in the four PPM inputs

Input What to enter Weight
Est. value (RM) The total project fee — what the client pays us. 40%
Client tier New client · Returning client · Key account (long-term strategic client) 25%
Margin target (%) Gross margin you're targeting on this project. Higher is better. 20%
Complexity (1–5) How hard is this project? 1 = straightforward, 5 = maximum effort. Lower is better in the score — simple work scores higher than complex work for the same fee. 15%

The score bar (0–100) updates in real time as you type. You don't need all four — two or more unlocks the score.

Step 2 — Read the quadrant recommendation

Quadrant is determined by just two factors: estimated value vs complexity. The threshold is RM 50,000 for value and 3 for complexity.

GOLD

High value (RM 50k+) · Low complexity (≤ 3)

The ideal project — good fee, manageable effort. Prioritise these.

STRATEGIC BET

High value (RM 50k+) · High complexity (4–5)

Worth doing — big fee justifies the effort. Needs strong resourcing.

OP. FILLER

Low value (< RM 50k) · Low complexity (≤ 3)

Fills studio capacity but don't prioritise over Gold or Strategic Bet.

DRAIN

Low value (< RM 50k) · High complexity (4–5)

Costs more than it returns. Requires exec + producer dual sign-off before proceeding.

Step 3 — Read the priority recommendation

Priority is derived from the weighted score (0–100), which combines all four inputs.

Score range Priority Meaning
70 – 100 P1 — High Top of the queue. Capacity should be allocated here first.
40 – 69 P2 — Medium Normal priority — schedule alongside other P2 work.
0 – 39 P3 — Low Fill-in work. Schedule when capacity allows.

Step 4 — Apply or override

Click "Apply suggestion →" to set both the quadrant and priority to the recommended values in one click. This saves immediately — no separate save button.

You can always override the recommendation — use the Quadrant and Priority dropdowns in the project fields to set them manually. The producer owns this decision.

If the recommendation already matches what's set, you'll see "✓ Quadrant and priority already match" — nothing to do.

Two recommendations — what's the difference?

PPM Recommendation

rule-based

Runs a fixed formula on your four inputs every time. Same inputs always give the same answer — no history, no learning.

  • ·Quadrant from two hard thresholds: value ≥ RM 50k and complexity ≤ 3
  • ·Priority from the weighted score (value 40 %, tier 25 %, margin 20 %, complexity 15 %)
  • ·Available immediately — works even on the very first project

AI Recommendation

learns over time

Looks at past producer decisions. Finds the 5 most similar past projects and asks: "What did the producer actually set on those?" That answer becomes the suggestion.

  • ·Needs at least 3 projects with PPM data before it activates
  • ·Gets more accurate as the project library grows
  • ·Learns from final decisions, not the formula — captures studio-specific patterns the formula can't know

When the two recommendations disagree — pay attention

A disagreement is a signal, not a bug. Examples of what it can mean:

→Formula says GOLD, AI says STRATEGIC BET — your team historically treats this type of project as harder than the complexity score suggests.
→Formula says P1, AI says P2 — similar projects have been deprioritised in practice, possibly because the client is high-maintenance or delivery risk is higher than the numbers show.

Rule: trust the formula when you have few projects. Trust the AI more as the library grows, especially when it disagrees — that disagreement often reflects real knowledge the formula doesn't have.

Confidence levels

HIGHA very close match was found (> 85% similar). Trust this recommendation.
MEDIUMReasonably similar projects found (60–85%). Good guidance but use judgement.
LOWNo close match found (< 60%). Treat as weak signal — rely on rule-based instead.

Privacy

All learning happens entirely within Pop OS using your own project data. No data is sent to any external service.

Drain gate — special approval required

When a project is classified as DRAIN (low value, high complexity), it requires two explicit approvals before production begins: an executive sign-off and a producer sign-off. These appear as checkboxes on the project detail page when the quadrant is set to DRAIN. Both boxes must be checked before work can be considered sanctioned. If the AI or rule-based engine recommends DRAIN, take it seriously — this project is likely to cost more than it earns.

Staff Suggestions (Project Detail)

Not to be confused with the Staffing Board (HR ▾ → Staffing) — that one ranks everyone by availability for one chosen week with no skill filtering. This is a different, skill-matched feature that lives on the project itself.

When a project is confirmed, the producer can tag the skills it needs directly on the project detail page. Pop OS then ranks every studio staff member by how well they match — combining skill level with available capacity during the project window.

How it works — step by step

1

Open the project detail page

Click any project name from the List, Kanban, or Timeline view, or from the Dashboard.

2

Tag required skills

In the Required Skills section, use the dropdown to add each skill the project needs (e.g. Rigging, Animation, Compositing). Skills are drawn from the master skill list managed in the People tab. Click × on a chip to remove a skill.

3

Read the Suggested Staff panel

The Suggested Staff section appears automatically. It shows up to 5 ranked candidates per skill, with a score (0–100), their skill rating (dots), and their free capacity percentage during the project window.

How candidates are scored

Factor What it measures Weight
Skill rating The person's current rating for that skill (1–5), as recorded in the People tab. Higher is better. 60%
Free capacity How much of their weekly capacity is unallocated during the project's start–deadline window. Green = 70 %+ free, yellow = 40–70 %, red = under 40 %. 40%

Score 0–100. A score of 100 means the person has a perfect skill rating (5/5) and is completely unallocated during the project period.

What the skill ratings mean (1–5)

Rating Level What it means
1 Awareness Knows the concept; has seen it done but hasn't produced independently.
2 Beginner Can attempt the task with guidance; output needs review.
3 Practitioner Delivers solid work independently; comfortable with standard complexity.
4 Advanced Handles complex work, mentors others, and raises quality on a project.
5 Expert Studio authority on this skill. Sets the standard, solves edge cases, pushes the craft forward.

Ratings are set by admin in the People tab (skill assignment panel). Each change is logged with a source — Interview, Project Completion, or Manual Adjustment (requires a written note).

Suggestions are per skill — a 3D Animation project might need Rigging, Animation, and Compositing, and each skill gets its own ranked list.

The system ranks staff who are not in the Warm Pool — i.e., active full-time, contract, or intern staff only.

Suggestions are a starting point, not a final decision. The producer still decides who to assign. Use the Capacity tab to make the actual allocation once you've chosen.

If a skill doesn't appear in the dropdown, it hasn't been added to the master list yet. Go to People tab → Skills to add it, then come back and tag the project.
